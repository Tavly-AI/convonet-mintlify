## What is the Agent Handbook?

The Agent Handbook is a curated library of ready-made prompt presets that instantly optimize your agent's communication style. Toggling a preset automatically injects best-practice instructions into the LLM context, eliminating the need to write complex manual prompts.

<Note> **Default Presets:** Every newly created agent automatically has the **Default Tone (Professional)** and **AI Disclosure When Asked** presets enabled to ensure a strong baseline experience.</Note>

The Handbook organizes presets into three primary operational categories:
* **Personality & Tone:** Dictates how the agent sounds, from professional constraints to high-empathy customer service.
* **Accuracy & Format:** Optimizes data extraction and formatting for voice agents (e.g., verifying names and numbers).
* **Trust & Safety:** Enforces strict compliance boundaries, ensuring the agent discloses it is an AI and refuses out-of-scope questions.

## How do you manage Presets?

You activate presets directly from the **Agent Handbook** panel located inside your agent's prompt settings. Simply toggle the desired behaviors on or off.

## Personality & Tone Presets

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Default Tone">
    Sets the absolute baseline speaking style for the agent.

    * **Professional (~480 tokens | Voice & Chat | Default):** Clear, polite, and strictly business. Enforces a structural "Acknowledge → Statement → Next Step" flow while minimizing robotic filler phrases (e.g., "Certainly!").
    * **Professional + Conversational (~910 tokens | Voice Only):** Natural, casual, and highly fluid. Forces short conversational turns, one question at a time, and parses numbers exactly how humans speak them. Ideal for sales and scheduling. Read the [Conversational Mode](/build/conversational-mode) guide for details.

    **Professional Example:** *"I understand this is frustrating — let me look into that for you."*
    **Conversational Example:** *"Morning's pretty open — nine or ten thirty. Afternoon works too if that's easier."*
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Natural Filler Words (~100 tokens)">
    **Available for:** Voice Only | **Default:** Disabled

    Injects localized filler words ("um," "uh," "you know") to make the agent sound less scripted. Fillers trigger roughly once every 2–3 sentences.
    
    * **Best for:** Sales, customer success, or casual outreach.
    * **Avoid for:** Formal, medical, or highly regulated environments.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="High Empathy (~70 tokens)">
    **Available for:** Voice & Chat | **Default:** Disabled

    Forces the agent to utilize highly empathetic language when appropriate, aggressively acknowledging concerns before transitioning to solutions.
    
    * **Best for:** Support routing, complaint handling, and de-escalation workflows.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Accuracy & Format Presets

<Note> **Platform Limitation:** Every preset in the Accuracy & Format category is strictly Voice-only. They will automatically disable if applied to a Chat agent.</Note>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Echo Verification (~190 tokens)">
    **Default:** Disabled

    Forces the agent to explicitly repeat critical details (names, phone numbers) back to the user to confirm accuracy. Uncommon names are spelled out letter by letter.
    
    * **Best for:** Appointment booking and strict data collection.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="NATO Phonetic Alphabet (~190 tokens)">
    **Default:** Disabled

    Mandates the agent use the NATO phonetic alphabet (A as in Alfa, B as in Bravo) with distinct pauses when spelling alphanumeric strings.
    
    * **Best for:** Confirming complex email addresses or reference IDs.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Speech Normalization (~910 tokens)">
    **Default:** Disabled

    Forces the LLM to rewrite structured data (dates, currency, phone numbers) into highly natural spoken formats before generating audio. For example, "$758.08" is output as "seven fifty-eight dollars and eight cents."

    <Tip>To convert text at the raw audio synthesis layer instead of the LLM layer, enable the `speech_normalization` toggle inside your `handbook_config`.</Tip>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Smart Matching (~110 tokens)">
    **Default:** Disabled

    Instructs the agent to gracefully accept minor ASR transcription errors when verifying names against a CRM (e.g., accepting "Brendon" when verifying "Brandon").
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Trust & Safety Presets

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="AI Disclosure When Asked (~30 tokens)">
    **Available for:** Voice & Chat | **Default:** Enabled

    Enforces strict transparency. If a user asks if they are speaking to a human, the agent will explicitly confirm it is an AI assistant.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Scope Boundaries (~60 tokens)">
    **Available for:** Voice & Chat | **Default:** Disabled

    Aggressively limits the agent to answering only queries explicitly covered by its prompt and Knowledge Base. It will actively refuse to guess outside of its scope.
    
    * **Best for:** Medical, financial, and legal deployments where hallucination is unacceptable.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I customize what a preset actually says?">
    No — each preset is a fixed, best-practice prompt that can't be edited directly. For custom instructions, write them into your agent's own prompt instead. See the [Prompt Engineering Guide](/build/prompt-engineering-guide) for tips on doing that well.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Will handbook presets conflict with my own custom prompt?">
    No, they're designed to work alongside your custom prompt. That said, if your prompt already covers similar ground to a preset — say, you've written your own empathy guidelines — it's worth disabling that overlapping preset to avoid inconsistent or contradictory behavior.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why are some presets greyed out on my chat agent?">
    Five presets — Natural Filler Words, Echo Verification, NATO Phonetic Alphabet, Speech Normalization, and Smart Matching — are built specifically for spoken interactions and simply don't apply to chat agents.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
