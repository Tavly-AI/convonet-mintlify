## What is Expressive Mode?

Expressive Mode controls acoustic delivery style, not just conversational text. When active, the engine dynamically injects acoustic tags—such as vocal warmth, sighs, strategic pauses, and word emphasis—directly into the dialogue stream.

<Note> **Architecture Requirement:** Expressive Mode strictly requires **Platform Voices**. Custom voices and external provider models (like ElevenLabs or Cartesia) lack the infrastructure to support expressive synthesis.</Note>

<Note> **Language Support:** Emotion tags currently function exclusively in 13 languages (English, Chinese, Japanese, German, French, Spanish, Korean, Arabic, Russian, Dutch, Italian, Polish, and Portuguese). If triggered in an unsupported language, the tag is silently discarded without causing an error.</Note>

## How do you enable Expressive Mode?

You can enable Expressive Mode and define exactly which emotional ranges your agent is allowed to access directly from the voice configuration panel.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a Platform Voice">
    Open your agent settings, click **Select Voice**, and pick an eligible Platform Voice.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Activate the Feature">
    Toggle on **Expressive mode**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Configure the Emotion Inventory">
    Under **Auto emotion tags**, select the specific expressions the model is permitted to use. The agent autonomously determines when to trigger them based on dialogue context. If you select zero tags, the agent freely synthesizes expressive inflections without being restricted to a fixed list.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What Tags and Vocalizations are supported?

Automatic tags are divided across three core acoustic categories: Emotions, Acoustic Effects, and Word Emphasis.

* **Emotions:** `empathetic`, `excited`, `happy`, `curious`, `surprised`
* **Acoustic Effects:** `sigh`, `clear throat`, `pause`, `long pause`
* **Word Emphasis:** Synthesizes heavy acoustic stress on critical terms.

The model applies tags sparingly, reserving them for contextually appropriate moments:
* *"\[empathetic] I'm so sorry — don't worry about the details, I'll sort it."*
* *"\[excited] It went through — you're officially in."*
* *"That form? \[sigh] Yeah, it trips everyone up. Let's just do it together now."*
* *"Let me take a look. \[pause] Okay, I see what happened."*
* *"The deadline is \[emphasis] next Friday, not this one."*

**Strict Boundaries:** The engine refuses to place tags around numerical digits, proper names, currency amounts, or timestamps. The agent will also refuse to trigger emotional tags purely because a user demanded it.

## How do you customize Tag Guidelines?

Tavly utilizes default behavioral logic to determine when a tag triggers. You can override this logic to restrict or loosen exactly when the LLM is allowed to generate specific emotions.

1. Open the **Override instruction prompt** configuration area.
2. Edit the guidance definitions (e.g., restrict `[excited]` solely to moments where a user completes a payment).
3. Revert your modifications at any time using **Reset to default**.

## How do you Manually Inject Tags?

In addition to the LLM automatically generating tags, you can hardcode specific delivery styles by embedding bracketed tags directly into your node instructions or standard greetings.

<Frame>
  <img src="https://mintcdn.com/retellai/1vPu7bH8sONSXkOB/images/expressive-mode/manual-tag.png?fit=max&auto=format&n=1vPu7bH8sONSXkOB&q=85&s=1b65e3be06c8616f882232daab60573f" alt="A node prompt with a manually placed [happy] tag" width="310" height="370" data-path="images/expressive-mode/manual-tag.png" />
</Frame>

For example, writing a greeting line as `[happy] Hi, nice to meet you.` explicitly forces the synthesis engine to voice that specific sentence with an upbeat delivery, completely bypassing standard LLM contextual inference.

## Implementation Advice

<Tip>
  Select a tightly focused collection of tags suited to your workflow rather than enabling all options. Pairing `empathetic` with `pause` works well for problem resolution, whereas `happy` and `excited` suit outbound notifications. Targeted palettes produce more believable dialogue.
</Tip>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What if I turn it on but choose no emotions?">
    The voice model retains overall expressive modulation using open-ended contextual inference rather than limiting itself to a predefined list. Selecting specific tags restricts generation strictly to that chosen group.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Will callers hear the tag names?">
    No. Tags act strictly as synthesis instructions and are stripped before audio rendering. They are never spoken aloud and do not appear in transcript logs delivered to callers.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
