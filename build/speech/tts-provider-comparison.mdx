## What TTS engines are supported?

Tavly provides integrated, keyless access to seven distinct speech synthesis engines: **Platform Voices** (Tavly's native engine), **MiniMax**, **Cartesia**, **Inworld**, **Fish Audio**, **ElevenLabs**, and **OpenAI**. Your selected provider defines voice timbre, latency, language support, and feature compatibility.

**Platform Voices represent the recommended baseline for telephony agents.** They are explicitly engineered for telephone audio codecs, handle [TTS fallback](/build/tts-fallback) autonomously, and serve as the exclusive engine for [Expressive Mode](/build/expressive-mode). Select external providers only when your agent demands a highly specific persona, uncommon dialect, or vendor-specific API integration.

Many core voice personas are available across multiple provider backends. The prefix on a voice identifier designates its serving engine (e.g., `minimax-Cimo` and `11labs-Cimo` share the same core vocal profile). This enables direct performance evaluation across providers while maintaining persona continuity.

## Provider Profiles

<Note> **Acoustic Assessments:** The profiles below reflect Tavly's internal benchmark data. For third-party technical evaluations, review the [Artificial Analysis Speech Arena](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/arena). Because audio performance fluctuates wildly across target languages, strictly test sample audio in the dashboard before deploying any third-party voice to production.</Note>

### Platform Voices
Tavly's proprietary voice catalog is engineered specifically for real-time phone calls. It offers natural handling of filler sounds, conversational cadence, and acoustic clarity across cellular and landline codecs.
* **Resilience:** Fully automated failover during infrastructure outages.
* **Exclusivity:** The only engine compatible with [Expressive Mode](/build/expressive-mode) tags.

### MiniMax
MiniMax delivers top-tier performance when articulating hyphenated strings (such as spelling out "W - O - R - D") and demonstrates highly consistent cadence.
* **Language Edge:** Extensive support for Asian dialects, including Cantonese (accessible exclusively through MiniMax and Platform Voices).
* **Drawback:** Delivery can occasionally exhibit a slightly more synthetic profile relative to Inworld or ElevenLabs.

### Cartesia
Cartesia produces highly natural prosody and scores among the lowest absolute generation latencies in public testing.
* **Accuracy:** Character spelling accuracy exceeds ElevenLabs in benchmark runs.
* **Drawback:** Pacing and pitch stability can fluctuate abruptly, and dialect localization is weak across regional accents.

### Inworld
Inworld routinely holds the top rank in blind listening evaluations. The `inworld-tts-2-flash` model significantly reduces response latency while trading only minor acoustic depth.
* **Language Edge:** Highly focused support for 15 core languages, including English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Hindi, and Arabic.

### Fish Audio
Fish Audio provides extensive linguistic coverage through its robust `s2-pro` and `s2.1-pro` architectures. The `s2-pro` checkpoint routinely ranks as the top open-weight architecture in the Artificial Analysis Speech Arena.

### ElevenLabs
ElevenLabs supplies a comprehensive voice library spanning numerous regional accents, heavily bolstered by community-contributed models.
* **Drawback:** Exact letter-by-letter spelling demonstrates lower precision.
* **Notice:** Previous Turbo checkpoints are [deprecated](/deprecation-notice/2026/07-12_elevenlabs_turbo_models) in favor of their Flash replacements. `eleven_flash_v2` is strictly restricted to English.

### OpenAI
OpenAI delivers broad language availability across both its `tts-1` and `gpt-4o-mini-tts` endpoints with identical multilingual parity. OpenAI voices **do not support** voice cloning.

## Selection Matrix

Use this matrix to rapidly identify the optimal TTS provider for your specific operational requirement.

* **Telephony-first deployments or default recommendation** → [Platform Voice](/build/platform-voices)
* **Top-ranked blind evaluation quality** → Inworld
* **Dynamic vocalizations, pauses, sighs, and emphasis** → Platform Voice with [Expressive Mode](/build/expressive-mode)
* **High spelling fidelity (alphanumeric codes, emails, names)** → MiniMax or Cartesia
* **Cadence and tempo consistency** → MiniMax
* **Cantonese or regional Asian dialects** → MiniMax or Platform Voice
* **Programmatic emotion setting via API** (`voice_emotion`) → Cartesia or MiniMax
* **Voice cloning with automated redundancy** → Clone to `platform` using the [Clone Voice API](/api-references/clone-voice)
* **Targeting specific languages** → Consult [Language support by provider](/build/language-support) to verify coverage

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need my own account or API key with these providers?">
    No. Tavly manages and hosts all seven text-to-speech integrations directly. You choose a voice in the dashboard or API, and Tavly handles vendor infrastructure, billing, and routing automatically without requiring third-party credentials.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if a provider has an outage during a call?">
    For Platform Voices, Tavly executes automatic internal failover without session disruption. When deploying third-party provider voices, define a [TTS fallback plan](/build/tts-fallback) to re-route calls to a designated backup provider in real time.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I switch providers without changing how my agent sounds?">
    Yes. Many voice models exist across multiple provider engines under identical persona labels (such as migrating from `cartesia-Cimo` to `minimax-Cimo`). Preview both models within the dashboard prior to switching, as exact timbre and rendering parameters differ slightly across vendor engines.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
