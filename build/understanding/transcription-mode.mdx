## What drives the Accuracy vs. Latency trade-off?

Real-time transcription inherently forces a strict trade-off: processing speed versus transcription precision. This dynamic is entirely dictated by how the system handles **interim results** and **endpointing** during live audio streaming.

<Note> **Architecture Constraint:** This guide applies exclusively to cascading agents. If you are deploying a native speech-to-speech model, this setting is ignored.</Note>

* **Interim Results:** These are early, partial transcripts generated while the caller is actively mid-sentence. They arrive instantly, yielding fast latency, but lack full sentence context, leading to higher baseline error rates.
* **Endpointing:** This defines the strict threshold of how long the transcriber waits after the caller stops speaking before locking in the final transcript. Higher endpoint thresholds provide maximum context for the model, yielding extreme accuracy, but at the direct cost of slower response times.

## What are the Transcription Modes?

Tavly allows you to explicitly configure how the ASR engine balances interim results and endpointing by offering two distinct transcription modes in the dashboard.

<img src="https://mintcdn.com/retellai/pRGcctz_zOqy0mSt/images/transcription-mode.jpeg?fit=max&auto=format&n=pRGcctz_zOqy0mSt&q=85&s=46447194022d07992db5fc65dc2b931d" width="254" height="108" data-path="images/transcription-mode.jpeg" />

* **Optimize for speed:** The system aggressively relies on the most recent interim results combined with a very low endpointing threshold. This instantly passes data downstream, achieving the absolute lowest possible latency.
* **Optimize for accuracy:** The transcriber holds its output, waiting for a much higher endpointing threshold before processing. Gathering this extended conversational context produces a highly accurate transcript, but incurs roughly **200ms** of additional latency.

## Which mode should you use?

Internal benchmarking proves both modes achieve a similar overall Word Error Rate (WER) for general conversation. The critical difference lies exclusively in capturing highly specific, complex entities.

* **Choose "Optimize for accuracy"** if your core use case relies heavily on extracting exact entities—such as long phone numbers, alphanumeric account IDs, or specific dates. The extra 200ms is necessary for the model to parse these correctly.
* **Choose "Optimize for speed"** for the vast majority of standard conversational use cases where raw speed and conversational fluidity are the highest priorities.
