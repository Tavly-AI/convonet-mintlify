Phone call testing connects your voice agent directly to public telephony networks (PSTN), allowing you to audit features that [web call](/test/test-web) sessions cannot evaluate: carrier-grade audio compression, mobile network latency, dual-tone multi-frequency (DTMF) keypad inputs, and live line transfers. Conduct a phone test as your final pre-flight verification before routing live customer calls.

<Note>
  Telephony testing requires assigning an active [phone number](/deploy/purchase-number) to your agent. When placing or receiving a test call, the platform executes whichever [agent version](/agent/version) is linked to that number: an explicit version number, an environment tag, <code className="ui-btn">Latest Published</code>, or <code className="ui-btn">Latest Created</code>. Because <code className="ui-btn">Latest Created</code> dynamically resolves to your most recent draft, you can test work-in-progress agents without publishing them first.
</Note>

## What is phone call testing?

**Phone call testing** connects your Tavly voice agent directly to public switched telephone networks (PSTN) for pre-production validation. This testing environment audits telecommunication capabilities that browser WebRTC sessions cannot evaluate, including carrier-grade audio codec compression, cellular network latency, dual-tone multi-frequency (**DTMF**) touch-tone keypad inputs, voicemail detection, and live call transfers.

Performing live phone testing ensures your agent performs reliably in noisy, low-bandwidth, and carrier-constrained mobile environments:

| Telecom Capability | Phone Call Testing (PSTN) | Web Call Testing (Browser WebRTC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Carrier Codecs & Latency** | Evaluates cellular jitter, packet loss & narrowband/wideband codecs | Uncompressed low-latency browser audio stream |
| **DTMF Keypad Input** | Native touch-tone digit detection for IVRs and PINs | Not supported (no telephone dialpad interface) |
| **External PSTN Transfers** | Validates live cold, warm, and agentic warm transfers | Fails (only agent-to-agent internal handoffs work) |
| **Answering Machine (AMD)** | Analyzes voicemail greetings and machine audio | Inactive |
| **Outbound SMS Dispatch** | Transmits SMS from assigned caller ID | Fails (lacks telephone number association) |

## How do you initiate an outbound test call?

To initiate an **outbound test call**, assign your agent as the designated Outbound Agent on an owned phone number, configure the target version, and trigger a dial to your personal phone. Ensure your workspace has completed **KYC identity verification**, then supply sample values for dynamic variables in the launch dialog.

<Note>
  To prevent toll fraud and regulatory violations, newer workspace accounts must complete [identity verification](/accounts/kyc) before Tavly enables outbound calling capabilities, including test calls. If your workspace has not satisfied KYC requirements, the telephone number drawer presents a verification banner rather than the call trigger button.
</Note>

Follow these structured steps to place an outbound test call:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Assign the outbound agent to a number">
    Navigate to the <code className="ui-btn">Phone Numbers</code> dashboard, select an owned number, and assign your agent as the designated <code className="ui-btn">Outbound agent</code>. Specify the target version to execute: a static version number, an environment tag, <code className="ui-btn">Latest Published</code>, or <code className="ui-btn">Latest Created</code> to test your unpublished draft.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Place the test call">
    Within the number settings panel, click <code className="ui-btn">Make an outbound call</code>, enter your destination phone number, and start dialing. Supply test values for any [dynamic variables](/build/context/dynamic-variables) in the launch dialog so session placeholders resolve accurately.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How do you test via inbound dialing?

To test via **inbound dialing**, bind your agent as the designated Inbound Agent on an owned phone number in the Tavly console and dial that number directly from a telephone. The telephony gateway immediately connects your call to the assigned agent version, allowing you to converse naturally and audit real-world acoustic performance.

Execute an inbound telephone test using the following steps:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Assign the inbound agent to a number">
    In the <code className="ui-btn">Phone Numbers</code> view, open an owned number and designate your agent as the <code className="ui-btn">Inbound agent</code>.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Dial the telephone number">
    Place a phone call to the assigned number from your mobile device or landline. The telephony gateway connects your call directly to the bound agent.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What should you verify in your telephony validation checklist?

A complete **telephony validation checklist** verifies telecommunication features that operate exclusively across carrier networks. Test DTMF touch-tone collection, confirm cold or warm line transfers, audit answering machine detection (**AMD**) for voicemails, ensure outbound SMS notifications deliver to real mobile devices, and evaluate speech clarity and conversational turn-taking under mobile network latency.

Audit the following capabilities during live phone verification:

* **DTMF digit collection**: Confirm that the agent detects touch-tone keypad presses and navigates menu trees accurately.
* **Telephony transfers**: Verify that cold transfers, warm handoffs, or agentic warm transfers complete successfully. External phone transfers cannot be simulated in browser audio.
* **[Voicemail](/build/handle-voicemail) and IVR machine detection**: Ensure the answering machine detection (AMD) pipeline identifies voicemail greetings or IVR systems and executes configured branch logic. Machine detection runs exclusively on PSTN calls.
* **Outbound SMS notifications**: Validate that mid-call SMS dispatch actions deliver messages to real devices.
* **Carrier audio codecs and network latency**: Evaluate speech clarity under cellular audio compression and verify how conversational turn-taking behaves under real-world network delay.

For instance, if an agent is designed to hand off billing inquiries to a support representative while confirming a numeric account PIN, dial into the agent, input digits on your phone keypad, and confirm that the transfer bridge executes cleanly without dropping audio.
